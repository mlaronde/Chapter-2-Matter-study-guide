Matter & Mass

Matter is anything that takes up space and has mass. Mass measures the amount of matter and is constant regardless of location. Weight depends on gravity.

Extensive vs Intensive

Extensive: depends on amount of matter — mass, length, volume.

Intensive: depends on type of matter — hardness, odor, density.

Pure Substances

A substance has a uniform, definite composition. Pure substances are classified as elements or compounds.

Elements

Simplest form of matter; cannot be broken down. Represented by 1–2 letter chemical symbols. If there are two letters, only the first is capitalized.

Compounds

Two or more different elements chemically combined in a specific ratio. Chemical formulas and subscripts show the ratio.

Physical Properties

Can be observed or measured without changing composition: hardness, color, conductivity, malleability, boiling point, and state of matter.

States of Matter

  • Solid: definite shape and volume; particles fixed closely together.
  • Liquid: definite volume, indefinite shape; particles close and able to flow.
  • Gas: indefinite shape and volume; particles far apart and moving quickly.

Vapor vs Gas

Vapor: gaseous state of a substance usually liquid or solid at room temperature. Gas: normally gaseous at room temperature.

Physical vs Chemical Change

Physical changes do not change identity: grinding, cutting, melting, boiling. Chemical changes form new substances.

Conservation of Mass

Mass is not created or destroyed. Mass of products = mass of reactants.

Mixtures

A mixture is a physical blend of two or more substances. Heterogeneous mixtures are not uniform; homogeneous mixtures are uniform and are also called solutions.

Separation Methods

Mixtures can be separated using physical properties: decanting, filtration, distillation, crystallization/evaporation, and chromatography.
